A Spoken Word

UNPAYABLE

On the grace we received — and the grace we owe
✦
I. THE ACCOUNT

Let me tell you about a debt.

Not a student loan — though I see you flinch —

// (Relax. We're not doing fees today. That's a different sermon.)

I mean a debt so deep, so wide, so tall,

that every bank on earth would drop the call.

The kind that breaks the ledger, bends the pen,

and leaves the accountant switching careers — again.

A debt that was yours. And mine.

Ours. All of ours. Every single line.

II. THE WEIGHT

We owed a death.

Not a metaphor — an actual, final breath.

Every lie whispered soft in the dark,

every prayer forgotten the moment you got signal bars.

// (You literally said "God if you get me out of this exam—" and then ghosted Him. He sees your read receipts.)

Every backstab dressed up as a joke,

every promise made and left to choke.

Stacked. Tallied. Owed.

And the only currency that heaven would take

was blood — sinless, spotless, perfect blood.

Nothing less. Nothing else. No substitute.

III. THE PAYMENT

And somehow — somehow —

before we could even spell repentance,

before we had accumulated the full audacity

of everything we'd eventually do,

He paid it.

Walked to a hill outside Jerusalem.

Stretched His arms wide — not like a man being crucified,

but like a father opening a door.

And bled.

Not reluctantly. Not with conditions.

Deliberately. Completely. For you.

Romans 5:8 —

while we were still sinners,

still neck deep, still smelling like the debt,

still refusing to make eye contact with the sky —

Christ died for us.

Not after we cleaned up.

Not after we got ourselves together.

Not after we stopped doing that one thing

we all know we keep doing.

Before. We. Deserved. It.

That is not a small grace.

That is an ocean given to people who were drowning in a puddle of their own making

— and calling it a lifestyle.

IV. THE IRONY

So here is where I need you to stay with me.

Because this is where it gets uncomfortable,

and I say that with love,

and also with the full awareness that you cannot leave

// (because Pastor is standing by the exit. Bless him.)

We — the forgiven, the redeemed, the washed —

have become the least forgiving people on campus.

We carry grudges like they're course units:

accumulating, compounding, and somehow never graduating.

// (Six semesters. Still enrolled in Bitterness 101. With distinction.)

We sit three tables away from someone who wronged us,

rehearsing the whole argument in our heads

while the rice goes cold

// (jollof rice, no less — and it deserves better than to be background noise to your unforgiveness.)

We let bitterness rent space in our chest,

furnish it, hang curtains, call it home —

and wonder why we can't breathe.

V. THE MATHEMATICS

Matthew 18. Let's do the math.

Ten thousand talents — that's the debt on your behalf.

Millions upon millions — cancelled, cleared, erased.

Wiped completely. Not deferred. Not restructured. Gone.

And then — and then —

you walked outside and grabbed your friend by the throat

over a hundred denarii — a few week's wages —

and said: "Pay me. Every. Last. Coin."

That's us. Most Fridays. In the group chat.

Your friend's offence — as real as it is —

is a decimal point

next to the mountain that was moved for you.

And you know it.

The Standard — Ephesians 4:32

Be kind to one another,

tenderhearted,

forgiving one another —

as God in Christ forgave you.

Not as the world forgives — conditionally, barely, with receipts.

As Christ forgave you.

That is the standard. That is the measure.

Not the minimum viable forgiveness.

The same grace. The same depth. The same love.

VI. THE MIRROR

See, grace was never meant to stop with you.

It was meant to flow through you — unstoppable, untameable, true.

You were not the end of the river.

You were always meant to be the channel.

When Christ said "forgive us our trespasses

as we forgive those who trespass against us" —

that wasn't poetry.

That was a mirror.

He was saying: the grace you've tasted,

the grace that found you at your worst and called you worthy —

give that away. Give it recklessly. Give it first.

// (Before they apologise. Before they change. Before they even know they hurt you. Yes, I said what I said.)

VII. THE BROTHER

You want to see it done? Let's talk about Joseph.

Sold by his own brothers — not strangers, not rivals —

the ones who ate at his father's table,

and decided a coat of many colors

was worth less than twenty pieces of silver.

// (Genesis 37. Family group chat, gone very, very wrong.)

Seventeen years old. Sold. Enslaved. Forgotten.

Falsely accused. Thrown in prison for a crime he didn't commit.

Helped a cellmate remember a dream —

and the cellmate forgot him. For two years.

// (Genesis 40:23. Even the guy he helped ghosted him. He knows about read receipts too.)

Thirteen years between the pit and the palace.

Thirteen years is enough time

to build a case, rehearse the speech,

plan exactly what you'll say

the day you finally have the power.

And when that day came — brothers bowing, begging for bread,

not even recognising the face they sold —

he didn't make them sweat.

No apology required first.

No groveling. No probation period.

"You meant evil against me, but God meant it for good."

Genesis 50:20

Just a man who'd already put the debt down

long before they ever asked him to.

That's not weakness.

That's a man who knew exactly

how much he'd already been forgiven for.

VIII. THE HARD PART

Now — I know.

I know what they did was real.

I know the wound is not aesthetic.

I know forgiveness is not the same as pretending.

Forgiveness doesn't mean what happened was fine.

It means you're no longer letting their wrong

define the rest of your time.

It means you open the fist.

You release the record.

You stop rehearsing the case

and you show them the same grace

that showed up at your door at your darkest place,

looked you dead in the eyes, and said —

"I choose you. Still. Anyway. Always."

Because bitterness is a chain that you forge link by link,

thinking it holds them —

but you're the one who can't swim because you won't let it sink.

// (Read that again. Slowly. I'll wait. Take your time.)

IX. THE CALL

So I'm asking you today — on this campus,

in this hall, in this season —

to become dangerous with grace.

Unreasonably forgiving.

Uncomfortably generous with mercy.

The kind of person people look at and say —

// ("Wait — they hurt you like THAT and you still…?" Yes. Yes they did. And watch.)

Because that is the testimony.

Not that you were never wronged,

but that you forgave the way you were forgiven —

wild, undeserved, and strong.

Let go because you were let go.

Show grace because grace found you low.

Extend mercy because mercy paid what you owe.

Give what was given. Flow where it flows.

The debt was real.

The cross was real.

The grace that cancelled everything you owed —

that was real.


Now let it be real in how you treat the person

sitting three tables away.


The same grace. Pass it on.

— Unpayable · A Spoken Word for the Campus · Selah
